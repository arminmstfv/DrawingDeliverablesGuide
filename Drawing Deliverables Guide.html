What belongs in each phase

Seven phases, from first meeting to post-occupancy. Pick one to see its purpose, the work you do, every deliverable it produces, and the approval that closes it.

Each phase builds on the one before it. Work done in a phase is presented to the client and approved before the next phase starts, and the cost estimate gets more precise each time: area × unit cost in programming, a statement of probable cost in SD, the parameter method in DD, detailed quantities in CD, and a firm price at bidding.

Sheet by sheet: SD, DD and CD content

Find a sheet type, open it, and read the column for the phase you are in. Items accumulate: each phase keeps the earlier content, updated, and adds detail.

How to read a sheet: the three columns come from a professional drawing-deliverables checklist. The grey From the NCIDQ manual box under each one summarizes what the NCIDQ Reference Manual says about that drawing: scale, content, who draws it, and exam points. Sheets marked NEW are interior-design sheets the manual covers that the original checklist did not.

Sheet codes (A0001, A01xx…) illustrate one numbering convention. The manual also describes discipline letter + group.sheet numbering (A1.1, A2.1, M1.1), which the worked example uses. Confirm specifics against your studio, instructor or firm standard.

SD

Schematic Design: big moves — concept, space plan, overall organization. Most detail sheets aren't started yet.

DD

Design Development: the plan gets fixed — dimensions, products, finishes, furniture, lighting, coordination with consultants.

CD

Construction Documents: full detail for bidding, permit and building — complete dimensions, schedules and references.

Go deeper: real drawings & U.S. standards

The sketches in this guide are simplified teaching diagrams. For the real thing — professionally drafted sheets and the standards U.S. practice is built on — start here.

Phase names follow the AIA sequence used across U.S. practice: programming, schematic design, design development, construction documents (AIA B152 calls this phase "contract documents"), bidding or negotiation, and contract administration. In Canada, bidding is called tendering and the punch list is the deficiency list.

NCIDQ content is summarized and paraphrased from the NCIDQ Interior Design Reference Manual, 7th edition (David Kent Ballast, PPI/Kaplan, 2021) for study use; chapter numbers refer to that edition. Code numbers in the worked example (occupant load factor, reach ranges, counter heights) reflect the IBC and 2010 ADA Standards as the manual presents them. Always check the code edition your jurisdiction has adopted.
